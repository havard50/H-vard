Artist Profile

About

Håvard Pedersen is a Norwegian guitarist, singer, and songwriter known for expressive tone, honest songs, and a live show that trades playback for pure nerve — blues-driven rock with soul and cinematic weight.

Long bio

Born in Vardø and now based in Oslo, Håvard has spent years refining a sound that sits between blues, classic rock, and melodic storytelling — always with the guitar voice up front and the arrangements built for real rooms.

Coming from a musical family, he is also the cousin of John Norum of Europe. That connection helped open doors early, but Håvard’s catalogue and stage identity stand on their own: raw, emotional, and unmistakably real.

Through projects like Cinematic World, The Norwegian Beatles, and Three Men Electric Jam, he has carried his music to audiences in the USA, England, Germany, France, Belgium, and Russia — with radio, television, and print coverage at home and abroad.

Review — Exit Music (UK)

February 21, 2011 · Jon Seymour · Never Felt So Good

“The album contains 14 tracks, 11 of which are original songs, and there are 3 absolutely brilliant cover versions.” On Thin Lizzy’s “Still In Love With You”, featuring John Norum on guitar and lead vocals: “In light of recent events, I can think of nothing else I’ve heard to be a more heartfelt tribute to the late, great Gary Moore. John Norum’s sound and vocal tone are suited perfectly to the song, and it was goosebump inducing.”

On the original material: “Really varied, touching on a lot of Håvard’s influences, including blues, rockabilly, pop/punk and a whole heap more… The sound is just so natural, nothing ever sounds forced… This is ‘feel good’ music of the highest order.”

“Go and have a poke around, and then buy this album, it’s frickin awesome!”

Norwegian press (English summaries)

August–November 2010 · Altaposten, NRK

Debut with a constellation of guests

Regional and national outlets reported on Håvard’s first solo album under the title Never Felt So Good (Cinematicworld Records), highlighting sessions in Alta with producer Kjetil Linnes and guest appearances including Europe guitarist John Norum, TNT’s Ronni Le Tekrø, and a wide circle of singers and players from rock, metal, and Sámi music scenes. Stories underlined how a holiday visit from Norum grew into studio work that helped push the project to completion.

“Good to finally get it out”

Altaposten followed the long road from first announcement to firm release date, describing a record built from both fresh songs and material that had waited years to be recorded. Håvard credited spontaneous takes and Linnes’s production instincts — including moments captured almost without him realizing the tape was rolling — as part of the album’s honest, unforced sound. Pieces also previewed heavy arrangements of covers such as The Pussycats’ “Just A Little Teardrop” and a broad, epic take on George Harrison’s “While My Guitar Gently Weeps”, in line with his role in The Norwegian Beatles.

NRK · Finnmark artist with an Europe connection

NRK framed the story for a national audience: a Finnmark-born guitarist releasing a debut with star-studded help, three covers among fourteen tracks, and a grounded outlook — a lifelong dream of making the record, without pop-star illusions.
