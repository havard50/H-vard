Background & story

Håvard Pedersen is a Norwegian guitarist, songwriter, and bandleader originally from the remote Arctic town of Vardø in Northern Norway. Growing up in a place where the raw forces of nature are impossible to ignore, he developed a deep emotional connection to music early on — drawn especially to the expressive power of the electric guitar.

Inspired by legendary artists like Gary Moore and Stevie Ray Vaughan, and bands like Kiss and Europe, Håvard picked up the guitar at a young age and quickly found his voice through blues and rock. A defining moment came when he experienced Europe live as a child — later forming a personal and musical connection through his cousin, guitarist John Norum.

Over the years, Håvard has built a solid reputation as a passionate and authentic live performer. With three albums and several singles released, his music reflects both personal experiences and universal themes — ranging from love and loss to resilience and transformation. His album You and I stands out as an emotional and introspective work, capturing the depth of his songwriting and melodic sensibility.

He has collaborated with notable musicians such as John Norum and Ronni Le Tekrø. Recent albums and singles are shaped in the studio with producer Peter Michelsen of Donkeyboy, recorded at The Norwegian Sound in Mjøndalen — a natural fit for his melodic, guitar-forward sound. He is an endorsed Gretsch artist (endorsed by Gretsch guitars). In addition to his solo career, he has been involved in several bands and projects including Cinematic World, The Norwegian Beatles, and Three Men Electric Jam — gaining extensive experience from stages across Norway and internationally in Europe, the UK, and the United States.

Today, Håvard fronts Håvard Pedersen & The Blues Is Alright Band, delivering what he calls “raw, real live music” — a no-nonsense, emotionally driven blues-rock experience rooted in authenticity. His playing is characterized by expressive phrasing, powerful tone, and a deep respect for the blues tradition, while still pushing toward a modern and personal sound.

Beyond the stage, Håvard is a creative entrepreneur with a strong independent spirit. After years in the retail and business world, he has chosen to focus fully on music and self-created projects — aiming to build a sustainable life around artistic freedom, live performance, and meaningful connection with audiences.

Despite facing personal challenges, including health setbacks and a major back surgery, he has remained committed to his path. His journey is one of persistence, reinvention, and belief in the power of music as both expression and purpose.

At his core, Håvard Pedersen is an artist driven not by trends, but by truth. His goal is simple: to create honest music, play powerful live shows, and build something real — on his own terms.

Review — Exit Music (UK)

February 21, 2011 · Jon Seymour · Never Felt So Good

“The album contains 14 tracks, 11 of which are original songs, and there are 3 absolutely brilliant cover versions.” On Thin Lizzy’s “Still In Love With You”, featuring John Norum on guitar and lead vocals: “In light of recent events, I can think of nothing else I’ve heard to be a more heartfelt tribute to the late, great Gary Moore. John Norum’s sound and vocal tone are suited perfectly to the song, and it was goosebump inducing.”

On the original material: “Really varied, touching on a lot of Håvard’s influences, including blues, rockabilly, pop/punk and a whole heap more… The sound is just so natural, nothing ever sounds forced… This is ‘feel good’ music of the highest order.”

“Go and have a poke around, and then buy this album, it’s frickin awesome!”

Norwegian press (English summaries)

August–November 2010 · Altaposten, NRK

Debut with a constellation of guests

Regional and national outlets reported on Håvard’s first solo album under the title Never Felt So Good (Cinematicworld Records), highlighting sessions in Alta with producer Kjetil Linnes and guest appearances including Europe guitarist John Norum, TNT’s Ronni Le Tekrø, and a wide circle of singers and players from rock, metal, and Sámi music scenes. Stories underlined how a holiday visit from Norum grew into studio work that helped push the project to completion.

“Good to finally get it out”

Altaposten followed the long road from first announcement to firm release date, describing a record built from both fresh songs and material that had waited years to be recorded. Håvard credited spontaneous takes and Linnes’s production instincts — including moments captured almost without him realizing the tape was rolling — as part of the album’s honest, unforced sound. Pieces also previewed heavy arrangements of covers such as The Pussycats’ “Just A Little Teardrop” and a broad, epic take on George Harrison’s “While My Guitar Gently Weeps”, in line with his role in The Norwegian Beatles.

NRK · Finnmark artist with an Europe connection

NRK framed the story for a national audience: a Finnmark-born guitarist releasing a debut with star-studded help, three covers among fourteen tracks, and a grounded outlook — a lifelong dream of making the record, without pop-star illusions.
